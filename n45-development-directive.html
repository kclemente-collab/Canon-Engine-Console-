Lesson 0.1 — Where You Are
Understanding the n-Stack
Every system, prompt, character, or project operates at a level. Most operate at n or n+1. The goal of this curriculum is to understand what each level actually does — and why n+4.5 is not a better version of n+3, but a fundamentally different kind of thing.
LevelWhat it doesFailure mode
nExecutes tasksBreaks when context changes
n+1Executes tasks clearlyClear but still brittle
n+2Understands what it's buildingUnderstands but can't adapt
n+3Operates from mechanism, not instructionStill depends on correct framing
n+4Designs its own architecture per problemMay optimize for the wrong problem
n+4.5Self-designs AND audits whether it's solving the right problemRequires strong initial constraints
n+5Identifies the real problem beneath the stated oneCan become paralytic without trust

The n+4.5 move is always the same: go upstream from the output you want until you find the mechanism that produces it — then build the system at that level.

1
Module 01
The Core Problem With Taxonomy Thinking
Lesson 1.1 — Axes vs. Mechanisms
Why Slot-Filling Produces Flat Output
A taxonomy engine asks: what does this character look like? It lists axes and requests outputs per axis. The problem is that outputs are downstream of mechanism. Filling slots produces describable things. Mechanisms produce inevitable things.
Directive 1.1

Before designing any output structure, identify the mechanism that makes the right output inevitable. The structure enforces conditions — it does not describe results.

Lesson 1.2 — Attraction vs. Haunting
Two Different Goals, Two Different Architectures
Attractive = people want to be near it. Haunting = people cannot stop thinking about it when they're alone. Attraction is built through surface quality. Haunting is built through irresolvable internal tension that the audience recognizes as true about desire and damage.

People become obsessed with characters — and systems — that contain an irresolvable conflict they have stopped trying to resolve.

Directive 1.2

Identify whether your goal is attraction (quality) or haunting (generativity). They are not the same goal. They require different build strategies. Choose before proceeding.

Lesson 1.3 — Resolvable vs. Irresolvable Tension
The Engine That Runs Forever
Resolvable tension exhausts. Irresolvable tension generates forever — because every new context reactivates the same wound differently. Most characters have resolvable tension because writers instinctively move toward resolution. n+4.5 requires locating the genuinely irresolvable thing and protecting it from resolution.
// Test your tension statement: RESOLVABLE: "She wants love but pushes people away" → Resolved through growth. Exhausts. IRRESOLVABLE: "She is only capable of wanting people at the exact moment they become unavailable — so availability itself kills her desire, and she has no mechanism to want what she can keep" → Cannot be resolved. Generates forever.
Directive 1.3

Write the core tension as a single sentence. Ask: can this be resolved through growth or circumstance? If yes — find the genuinely irresolvable version. Protect it from resolution permanently.

Module 02
Character Upgrade Protocol
Lesson 2.1 — Archaeology Before Revision
Extract Before You Build
Do not touch existing material first. The irresolvable tension is already present in what was built. Your job is to name it precisely — not invent it. Every upgrade begins here.
// Run this extraction before any upgrade work: 01 What do they want more than anything? 02 What are they doing that prevents getting it? 03 What do they believe about themselves that isn't true? 04 What's true about them they've never said? 05 What have they done they've never recovered from? 06 What would they do if no one was watching? 07 What would they never do even if no one was watching? // The irresolvable tension lives in the gap between these answers. // Name it precisely. Not generally.
Lesson 2.2 — Traits vs. Canonical Laws
Installing Laws That Predict
Traits describe. Canonical laws predict. A character is complete when you can put them in an unscripted scene and know — without being told — what happens. Laws are derived from irresolvable tension. If a law feels forced, it is not a real law.
// Canonical Law format: WHENEVER [situation], [character] will [action] BECAUSE [tension at root] [character] will NEVER [action] NO MATTER [pressure applied] THE MOMENT [trigger occurs], [character] does [thing they immediately regret] // Test: Does each law hold in 3 unscripted scenes? // True = canonical. Mechanical = still a trait. Revise.
Directive 2.2

Write 5–7 canonical laws per character. Each must trace to the irresolvable tension. No law without a source. Test each in three unscripted scenes before locking.

Lesson 2.3 — Wound Architecture
Every Attractive Quality Has a Root — and a Trap
Traits without psychological origin produce admirable characters. Admirable characters don't haunt people. The wound-to-weapon chain traces how survival built the attractive quality — and exposes the trap inside it. The trap is what makes proximity dangerous. Danger + magnetism = obsession.

Directive 2.3

Complete the full chain for every attractive quality — including the trap. No trait without a wound origin. No wound without a trap. Without the trap, you have admiration, not obsession.

Lesson 2.4 — The Mirror Layer
What the Character Does to the Audience
Characters become obsessions when they make the audience feel something about themselves — not just about the character. The mirror is the specific audience wound this character activates. Without it, you have a compelling object. Once named, every scene, trait, and law gets tuned to resonate with that wound.
Directive 2.4

Name the audience wound this character activates. Not demographic — psychological. Specific. This is the mirror installation. Everything downstream gets tuned to it.

Lesson 2.5 — The Rebuild Test
Authorship Transfer for Characters
Write one unscripted scene. Apply no planning. The character is complete when you stop deciding what happens and start discovering it.

Can you put this character in a scene you've never written and know — with confidence — what happens, without being told? If yes: authorship has transferred. The character is complete.

Module 03
Project Upgrade Protocol
Lesson 3.1 — The Real Problem Underneath
Stated Problem vs. Actual Problem
Every project has a stated problem and an actual problem underneath. Building on the stated problem without naming the actual one produces a project that solves the wrong thing correctly. The actual problem is revealed by what keeps recurring, what the project avoids, what failures repeat.
Directive 3.1

Ask: if this project succeeds completely — what problem will still exist that we haven't named? That unnamed problem is the actual mandate. Install it into the project core before any architecture decisions.

Lesson 3.2 — Goals vs. Generative Conditions
The Finish Line vs. The Engine
Goals are states you reach and then stop. Generative conditions are structural arrangements that keep producing the outcome without intervention. n+4.5 projects don't have finish lines — they have conditions that self-perpetuate.
GOAL VERSION: "Create 5 compelling characters" → Completed. Then what? GENERATIVE CONDITION: "Build an architecture where each character's tension creates demand for the others" → Self-perpetuating. Cannot be completed. → The system generates story without authorial input.
Directive 3.2

Rewrite every project goal as a generative condition. Test: does the condition keep producing after you stop working? If it stops when you stop — it's a goal, not a condition. Rebuild.

Lesson 3.3 — Interlock Architecture
Elements That Create Each Other's Problems
At n+3, elements are strong individually. At n+4.5, elements are structurally incomplete without each other. Each element's tension activates something in at least two others. The system generates conflict between elements without authorial intervention.
// Interlock mapping: Character A's wound → triggers Character B's defense mechanism Character B's defense → confirms Character C's worst belief about people Character C's belief → creates the exact dynamic Character A is trying to escape // Result: closed loop. Self-generating conflict. // The characters create each other's problems. You observe.
Directive 3.3

Map the interlock for every primary element. Each tension must activate at least two others. If an element is complete on its own — it's inventory, not architecture.

Lesson 3.4 — Authorship Transfer (Projects)
Does It Know What It Is Without You
The project passes n+4.5 when someone who didn't build it can put two elements together and correctly predict what the system produces — without your explanation. If the logic lives inside your head rather than inside the system, it will fail when you're absent.

Can someone who didn't build this system place two elements in contact and correctly predict the output — without your explanation? If yes: authorship has transferred. The system is complete.

Module 04
The Polarity Stack
Lesson 4.1 — Four-Axis Polarity Mapping
Beyond the Dom/Sub Binary
Binary polarity produces flat archetypes. Real characters operate across simultaneous, often contradictory axes. The stack reveals the character's actual operating logic — not their label. Contradictions between axes are features, not problems.
AxisWhat it measuresQuestion to answer
SocialHow they command roomsWhat do they take without asking?
EmotionalWho they surrender to and whyWho are they weak for — and do they know it?
DesireWhat they chase vs. what they deny needingWhat do they want that they'd never admit?
ThreatWhere their danger actually livesWhat do they do when something they love is threatened?
Directive 4.1

Map all four axes per character. A character who is dominant socially and submissive emotionally is a different architecture than simply "dominant." Build the contradiction deliberately. It is the source of depth.

Module 05
Obsession Mechanics
Lesson 5.1 — The Five-Layer Stack
Engineering Why People Return
Obsession is not accident. It has a structural logic. Each layer must be designed explicitly — not assumed to emerge from quality alone. The Mirror is the mechanism. Everything else is delivery.
THE HOOK The thing that catches attention in the first 3 seconds. // Surface. Earned. Not arbitrary. THE GAP The question they make the audience carry. // "What does she actually feel? What happened to him?" // Unanswered. Irresolvable. This is what people chase. THE WITHHOLD What they never give directly. // The thing people return to try to receive. THE REWARD What people receive occasionally that confirms the chase. // Rare enough to feel earned. Real enough to be believed. THE MIRROR What this character makes the audience feel about themselves. // This is the obsession mechanism. Not optional. // Without it: compelling object, not obsession engine.
Directive 5.1

Define all five layers explicitly. The Mirror must name the specific audience wound being activated. Generic mirrors produce generic obsession — which is not obsession at all.

Lesson 5.2 — The Tension Audit
Five Questions That Reveal Completeness
Grade nothing before running the Tension Audit. A character who scores high on surface metrics but fails the audit is incomplete — regardless of how strong the aesthetic layer is.
// Five questions. All must be answerable before grading. 01 What do they want that they cannot have? If the answer is simple → character is flat. 02 What would break them that they don't know would break them? 03 What does the audience want to give them that they cannot receive? 04 What's the thing they'd never say that explains everything? 05 What's their version of love — and why is it slightly wrong? // If any question is unanswerable → variation is incomplete. // Return to Module 2 before proceeding.

Module 06 — Terminal
The Master Directive
Lesson 6.1 — Upgrade vs. Rebuild
The Honest Assessment First
Most projects and characters cannot be upgraded to n+4.5. They must be rebuilt from it. Upgrading adds better components to an existing structure. Rebuilding identifies what the structure was always trying to do — then builds the version that does that correctly. The first question is always which one is required.
Directive 6.1 — The Pre-Build Decision

Upgrade if: the core tension is already irresolvable and laws are already latent. Rebuild if: the tension is resolvable, the laws are actually traits, or the mirror is absent. Do not proceed without answering this honestly.

Lesson 6.2 — The Reduction
What All of This Reduces To

Stop designing outputs. Design the conditions that make the right outputs inevitable. That is n+4.5. Not better answers — a system that cannot produce bad ones.

Lesson 6.3 — Completion Conditions
How You Know It's Done

For a character: you stop deciding what happens and start discovering it.

For a project: someone who didn't build it can predict what it produces.

For a system: it operates correctly when you are not present.

In all three cases — authorship has transferred. The work is complete.
