## STAGE 2 — DECONSTRUCTION PROTOCOL

When your team identifies a concept worth studying,
run it through this exact framework.

### THE 7-LAYER TEARDOWN

```
LAYER 1 — SILHOUETTE
Strip everything. Just the outline.
Ask: what does this say in pure shape?
Document: dominant proportion, roofline
character, overhangs, stance

LAYER 2 — SURFACE LANGUAGE
How does light move across the body?
Concave or convex dominant?
Where are the tension lines?
How many distinct surface planes?
Document: flow direction, panel count,
primary highlight path

LAYER 3 — MATERIAL SIGNAL
What materials are implied or shown?
What does the surface treatment say
about the world this car lives in?
Document: paint philosophy, trim language,
wheel design vocabulary

LAYER 4 — LIGHTING SIGNATURE
Headlight and taillight graphic.
This is the face. It's the most copied
and most distinctive element.
Document: graphic type (blade/ring/pixel/
organic), light source direction,
signature shape

LAYER 5 — AERO EXPRESSION
Is aerodynamics visible or hidden?
Active or passive?
Does it look functional or theatrical?
Document: vent count, wing presence,
diffuser expression, overall aero philosophy

LAYER 6 — INTERIOR PHILOSOPHY
Cockpit or cabin?
Driver-focused or experience-focused?
What does the steering wheel say?
Document: driver orientation, HMI approach,
material story inside vs outside relationship

LAYER 7 — CULTURAL VECTOR
What cultural moment is this responding to?
What anxiety or aspiration does it address?
What does owning this say about the owner?
Document: cultural positioning,
aspirational identity, fear/desire it resolves
```

## STAGE 3 — TREND MAPPING

After deconstructing 5+ concepts,
map them across these axes to find the pattern.

### THE TREND MATRIX

```
AXIS 1 — ORGANIC vs GEOMETRIC
Where does each concept sit?
Plot it 1-10.
Watch which direction the cluster moves
over 12-24 months.
That movement is the trend.

AXIS 2 — VISIBLE vs HIDDEN TECHNOLOGY
Is tech displayed or concealed?
Exposed carbon vs smooth skin?
Visible aero vs clean surface?

AXIS 3 — AGGRESSION vs ELEGANCE
Not either/or — where on the spectrum?
The interesting concepts live
in the unexpected zone.

AXIS 4 — CULTURAL ORIGIN
Which design culture is this from?
Italian (emotional surface)
German (tension and precision)
Japanese (restraint and detail)
American (scale and statement)
British (character and eccentricity)
Korean (emerging — synthesis culture)

AXIS 5 — TIME ORIENTATION
Is this nostalgic, present, or future?
Which decade is it in dialogue with?
```

### WHAT TO LOOK FOR
```
CONVERGENCE: multiple studios moving
             the same direction simultaneously
             → a trend is forming

OUTLIER:     one studio going opposite direction
             → either wrong or 5 years early
             → investigate which

GAP:         a combination no one is exploring
             → opportunity space

EXHAUSTION:  a direction everyone has done
             → ready for inversion
```

## STAGE 4 — N+5 EVOLUTION ENGINE

This is the methodology for generating
what doesn't exist yet.

### THE EVOLUTION LADDER

Take any identified concept and run it
through five evolution steps.

```
CURRENT STATE (n)
What exists now. Document precisely.

n+1 — REFINEMENT
Same direction, executed better.
Remove what doesn't work.
Strengthen what does.
"What if this was more itself?"

n+2 — EXTENSION
Push the dominant characteristic
to its logical conclusion.
If it's aggressive — more aggressive.
If it's organic — more organic.
"What if this went all the way?"

n+3 — SYNTHESIS
Take the dominant characteristic
and combine it with its opposite.
Aggressive + elegant.
Organic + geometric.
"What if this contained its contradiction?"

n+4 — INVERSION
Flip the core premise entirely.
If it hides technology — expose everything.
If it's low — make it tall.
"What if the opposite were true?"

n+5 — CULTURAL TRANSPLANT
Take the design DNA and place it
in a completely different cultural context.
Japanese concept + American scale.
Italian surface language + German engineering philosophy.
"What if this came from somewhere else?"
```

### DOCUMENTING EACH STEP
```
For each n+ step record:
— Form description (3 sentences max)
— Surface language (1 sentence)
— Cultural vector (who buys this and why)
— Slider values: AGG / CURV / AERO / STANCE / GLOSS
— One-line concept statement
```

## STAGE 5 — NEW BREED CONCEPTUALIZATION

This is where research becomes original work.

### THE BREED GENERATOR

A new breed emerges from combination
of things that haven't been combined.

```
STEP 1 — IDENTIFY THREE INPUTS
One from automotive trend research
One from outside automotive entirely
One from FGE constitutional aesthetic

Example:
→ Trend: extreme aero visibility (auto)
→ Outside: deep sea creature morphology (biology)
→ FGE: kintsugi fracture language (constitutional)

STEP 2 — FIND THE UNIFYING LOGIC
What do these three things share?
What underlying principle connects them?

Example above:
→ All three: structural integrity made visible
   The skeleton IS the surface.
   Strength displayed not hidden.

STEP 3 — NAME THE BREED
One phrase. Not descriptive — evocative.
The name should make someone want
to see it before they've seen it.

Example: "Exoskeletal"

STEP 4 — WRITE THE CONCEPT STATEMENT
Two sentences maximum.
What it is. What it means.

Example:
"A hypercar where the structural
 architecture IS the body surface.
 Strength displayed as beauty —
 the skeleton and the skin are one."

STEP 5 — GENERATE THE BRIEF
Feed into Supercar Forge with:
— New archetype name
— Slider values derived from concept
— Paint shader that matches the logic
— Environment that reveals the concept
— FGE constitutional layer
```
